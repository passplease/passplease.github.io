## 我的猜想
最初我注意到作者原本的证明[^proof]在证明全部局面最大值是$\omega_1$的时候使用了$\omega_1$的正则性，但是我们都知道$ZF$并不保证$\omega_1$是正则的，只有选择公理给出这个保证，但是没有选择公理就不一定了。于是我开始考虑有没有可能局面数能够达到$\omega_1$，如果我们去除选择公理？

---
当然，这很扯，而且证明最后也否定了我的猜想，可是虽然猜想被否定，但他给出了一些更奇特的结论，下面我会从证伪猜想入手。
## 猜想的证伪
直接说最终思路，首先我们需要得到几个命题的语句性质，下面我们先规定基础记号，然后给出几个语句，并给出他们的$\Sigma_1$或者$\Delta_0$性质。
### 基础记号
这部分可能需要结合[表达式](#常用表达式)一起看
#### 合法局面$T$
全部合法的局面的集合

$$
T=\left\{(a,0),(a,1)\;\middle|\quad\begin{aligned} &a\subset\omega\times\omega\times7\times2\;\wedge\\ &a是一个\omega\times\omega\to7\times2的函数\;\wedge\\&\forall a_1,a_2\in a(\operatorname{rng}(a_1)=(0,0)\wedge\operatorname{rng}(a_2)=(0,0)\to a_1=a_2)\wedge\\&\forall a_1,a_2\in a(\operatorname{rng}(a_1)=(0,1)\wedge\operatorname{rng}(a_2)=(0,1)\to a_1=a_2)\wedge\\&\exists a'\in a(\operatorname{rng}(a')=(0,0)\vee\operatorname{rng}(a')=(0,1))\end{aligned}\;\right\}
$$

其中第一个7表示棋子种类，具体种类不重要，不影响我们的证明，此处取0代表王。而第二个2表示黑白的棋子，以0代表白方，与$a$配对的那个则是表示目前是谁走棋，0表示白方走棋。最后三条是说，白方只能存在一个王，黑方也是，同时场上至少有一个王，一个王对应另一个王已经被击杀了嘛。<br><br>
此外我们还定义$T_0$表示全部只有一个王的局面。<br><br>
注意到$\omega$是$\Sigma_1$可定义的，那么很明显，$T$与$T_0$都是$\Sigma_1$可定义的。
#### 具体局面$p,q$
以$p,q\in T$记每一个具体的局面

---
接下来是几个最常用的表达式
### 常用表达式
#### 后继局面$A$
对于局面$p,q$，以符号$A$记$q$是$p$的后继局面，同时我们不妨假设这一步是白棋走棋，也就是当前局面是$(p,0)$，下一步是$(q,1)$，表达式如下

$$
A((p,0),(q,1))=\exists q'\in q\,(|q-p|=\{q'\})\wedge
\left(\begin{aligned}
&\left(\begin{aligned}
&\exists\{p_1',p_2'\}\subset p\,(|p-q|=\{p_1',p_2'\})\wedge\\
&p_1'是白棋\wedge p_2'是黑棋\wedge\\
&q'与p_1'是同一种棋，且p_1'走到q'是合法的\wedge p_2'与 q'位置相同
\end{aligned}\right)\\
&\vee\\
&\left(\begin{aligned}
&\exists p'\in p\,(|p-q|=\{p'\})\wedge\\
& q'与p'是同一种棋，且p'走到q'是合法的
\end{aligned}\right)
\end{aligned}\right)
$$

虽然我这里用了一些中文，但是应该不影响大家阅读还有定义的严谨性，这些中文肯定是$\Delta_0$可定义的一些性质。同时此处假设是白方走棋也完全可以是黑方走棋，都类似，不再赘述<br><br>
很明显，这个性质是$\Sigma_1$可定义的。
#### 必胜判据$\psi$
递归地对所有局面$p\in T$定义其是白方必胜或黑方必胜或胜负不定<br><br>
对于全部只有一个王的局面$p\in T_0$，初始地对应定义其胜负，若黑方必胜则$\psi(p)=1$，若白方必胜则$\psi(p)=0$，递归地，对集合

$$
T_1^0=\begin{aligned}&\{(q,0)\mid\exists p\in T_0,A((q,0),p)\wedge\psi(p)=0\}\cup\\
&\{(q,1)\mid\forall p\in T_0(A((q,1),p)\to\psi(p)=0)\}\end{aligned}
$$

我们也可以相应定义$p\in T_1^0$中的全部局面都是白方必胜$\psi(p)=0$，同时还有

$$
T_1^1=\begin{aligned}&\{(q,1)\mid\exists p\in T_0,A((q,1),p)\wedge\psi(p)=1\}\cup\\
&\{(q,0)\mid\forall p\in T_0(A((q,0),p)\to\psi(p)=1)\}\end{aligned}
$$

这些局面也都是黑方必胜$\psi(p)=1$的。如此反复定义就能得到全部必胜局面，而对于还没有涉及到的局面，我们就可以定义他们都是$\psi(p)=2$的，也就是胜负不定。<br><br>
同时我们不必处理极限情况，因为不管任何局面只要是必胜的，都必须在有限步内终止，你是可以在有限步内走到各个极限步骤上的，这是序数良序的结果。<br><br>
不必多说，由此定义的$\psi$是$\Sigma_1$可定义的。
> 值得注意的是，递归本身就会有复杂度的影响，不过此处我们的递归只在$\omega$上，最多也就是一个$\Sigma_1$复杂度，构不成很大的干扰。不过在后面定义局面数的时候递归自己的复杂度干扰就会造成很大影响了

#### 后继局面树$T_p$
对白方必胜局面$\psi(p)=0$定义从局面$p$开始走向胜利的树，首先递归定义序列，$T_p^0=\{(p)\}$，假定$T_p^i$已经有定义

$$
T_p^{i+1}=\{(\textbf i,q)\mid\textbf i=(p,p_1,\cdots,p_i)\in T_p^i\wedge q\in T\wedge A(p_i,q)\wedge\psi(q)=1\wedge\forall s\leq i(p_i\neq q)\}
$$

最后取

$$
T_p'=\{s\mid\exists i\in\omega(s\in T_p^i)\wedge s_i\in T_0\}
$$

明显的，$T_p'$实际上就是$p$能走到胜利的全部走法，因为每一个具体的走法必须在有限步结束，所以收集是全的，同时还排除了“来回踱步”，接下来就是把他重新组织为一棵树。<br><br>
现在来定义偏序关系$<$，定义树上的元素集合$T_p^*$

$$
T_p^*=\{(q,s\upharpoonright_i)\mid s\in T_p'\wedge\exists i\in\operatorname{dom}(s)\wedge s(i)=q\}
$$

然后定义$<$如下

$$
(q,s)<(m,r)\leftrightarrow s\subset r\wedge q\in\operatorname{rng}(r)
$$

显然有$(p,\varnothing)$是最小元素，且对全部小的元素是良序的，如此便能组织为一棵树。<br><br>
自然，这是$\Sigma_1$可定义的。
#### 局面数$\operatorname{cf}$
这个是每一个局面$p\in T$如果是白方必胜$\psi(p)=0$的时候，胜利最少所需要的步数。使用$T_p$就能很容易定义，从顶部节点开始（是可以找到全部顶部节点的），这些节点都是$T_0$中的，他们的$\operatorname{cf}$都是0。递归地定义上游节点$(q,s)$，如果$q$的全部后继节点都已经定义了$\operatorname{cf}$则也可以定义$q$了
- 若$q$是白方走棋则定义$\operatorname{cf}(q)=\bigcap\limits_{\forall r\in\operatorname{dom}T_p(A(q,r))}\operatorname{cf}(r)+1$
- 若$q$是黑方走棋则定义$\operatorname{cf}(q)=\bigcup\limits_{\forall r\in\operatorname{dom}T_p(A(q,r))}\operatorname{cf}(r)$

由$T_p$的定义，如果还有一个节点是$(q,r)$，他的$\operatorname{cf}$还会与$(q,s)$一致，所以可以记作$\operatorname{cf}(q)$忽略$s$<br><br>
很明显，$\operatorname{cf}$是$\Sigma_1$可定义的。
>实际上这个地方递归造成了许多影响，不过都使用对树$T_p$的定义绕过了，此处最难的就是合理得对所有局面$p$

---
至此准备工作完成，接下来就是证明的主体
### 证明
首先考虑$ZF$的模型$V$以及其内模型$L$，因为$L$是$ZFC+GCH$的模型，很明显可以证明命题

$$
\varphi: \forall p\in T,\|\operatorname{cf}(p)\|=\omega
$$
不过我们注意到，上述语句$\varphi$是$\Sigma_1$可定义的，唯一值得提到的是势的定义，此处是说与$\omega$等势，也就是存在一个一一映射将$\|\operatorname{cf}(p)$映射到$\omega$，显然是$\Sigma_1$的。而在$L$中，如果一个$\Sigma_1$语句为真，那么在$V$中对应的语句也必须为真，也就是说在$V$中语句$\varphi$依然必须为真，从而在任何$ZF$的模型中我们都有一个局面的局面数必须是与$\omega$等势的，从而就不可能达到$\omega_1$，证毕。

---
根据上面的证明我们可以发现一些令人震惊的事实，也就是局面数可数这件事并不依赖$\omega_1$的正则性（也就是作者的证明[^proof]），同时这个上界$\omega_1$也不会因为$CH$被破坏而上升，不管任何情况下，不论有没有连续统假设，他都会是$\omega_1$，不会变大一点。

# An Exploration of the Upper Bound in Infinite Chess
## Note on terminology
## My conjecture
I first noticed that the author’s original proof[^proof] uses the regularity of $\omega_1$ when proving that the maximum value of all positions is $\omega_1$. But $ZF$ does not guarantee that $\omega_1$ is regular, only the Axiom of Choice gives this guarantee. Without the Axiom of Choice, it might not be held. So I began to consider whether it is possible for the number of positions, or rather for game values, to reach $\omega_1$ if we remove the Axiom of Choice and make $\omega_1$ not regular any more.<br><br>
Of course, this is rather absurd, and the proof eventually disproved my conjecture. But although the conjecture was refuted, it led to some more unusual conclusions. Below I will begin by trying to disprove the conjecture.
## Disproof of the conjecture
In order to state my proof, firstly we need to define some symbols. Below we first fix the basic notation, then give several statements and their $\Sigma_1$ or $\Delta_0$ properties.
### Basic notation
This part may need to be read together with [Common expressions](#common-expressions).
#### Legal positions $T$
The set of all legal positions is

$$
T=\left\{(a,0),(a,1)\;\middle|\quad\begin{aligned} &a\subset\omega\times\omega\times7\times2\;\wedge\\ &a\text{ is a function }\omega\times\omega\to7\times2\;\wedge\\&\forall a_1,a_2\in a(\operatorname{rng}(a_1)=(0,0)\wedge\operatorname{rng}(a_2)=(0,0)\to a_1=a_2)\wedge\\&\forall a_1,a_2\in a(\operatorname{rng}(a_1)=(0,1)\wedge\operatorname{rng}(a_2)=(0,1)\to a_1=a_2)\wedge\\&\exists a'\in a(\operatorname{rng}(a')=(0,0)\vee\operatorname{rng}(a')=(0,1))\end{aligned}\;\right\}.
$$

Here the first $7$ represents the piece types. The exact types do not matter and do not affect our proof, and here $0$ represents the king. The second $2$ represents the color of the piece, with $0$ representing White. The component paired with $a$ indicates whose turn it is: $0$ means it is White’s turn. The last three formulae say that White can have only one king, and likewise Black. And also there is at least one king on the board, obviously if only one king is present, the other has already been captured.<br><br>
In addition, let $T_0$ be the set of all positions with exactly one king.<br><br>
Note that $\omega$ is $\Sigma_1$-definable, so both $T$ and $T_0$ are $\Sigma_1$-definable.
#### Concrete positions $p,q$
Let $p,q\in T$ denote a certain positions.

---

Next are several of the most commonly used expressions.
### Common expressions
#### One-step legal move $A$
For positions $p,q$, let $A(p,q)$ means that $q$ is obtained from $p$ by one legal move. For example, if White is to move, the current position is $(p,0)$ and the next position is $(q,1)$. The expression is as follows:

$$
A((p,0),(q,1))=\exists q'\in q\,(|q-p|=\{q'\})\wedge
\left(\begin{aligned}
&\left(\begin{aligned}
&\exists\{p_1',p_2'\}\subset p\,(|p-q|=\{p_1',p_2'\})\wedge\\
&p_1'\text{ is White to move}\wedge p_2'\text{ is Black to move}\wedge\\
&q'\text{ is the same type as }p_1',\ \text{and the move from }p_1'\text{ to } q'\text{ is legal},\ \text{and }p_2'\text{ is on the same square as }q'
\end{aligned}\right)\\
&\vee\\
&\left(\begin{aligned}
&\exists p'\in p\,(|p-q|=\{p'\})\wedge\\
& q'\text{ is the same type as }p',\ \text{and the move from }p'\text{ to } q'\text{ is legal}
\end{aligned}\right)
\end{aligned}\right)
$$

Although I have used some informal phrases here, this should not affect readability or the rigor of the definitions. these phrases are all some $\Delta_0$-definable properties. Also, although we assumed here that White is to move, it could be Black and definable in the same. The cases are analogous and no need to be repeated.<br><br>
Clearly, this property is $\Sigma_1$-definable.
#### Winning/Draw formula $\psi$
Recursively define, for every position $p\in T$, whether it is winning for White, winning for Black, or drawn/undefined.<br><br>
For all positions $p\in T_0$ with exactly one king, initially define their outcome accordingly: if Black wins, then $\psi(p)=1$; if White wins, then $\psi(p)=0$. Recursively, for the set

$$
T_1^0=\begin{aligned}&\{(q,0)\mid\exists p\in T_0,A((q,0),p)\wedge\psi(p)=0\}\cup\\
&\{(q,1)\mid\forall p\in T_0(A((q,1),p)\to\psi(p)=0)\}\end{aligned}
$$

we can correspondingly define all positions $p\in T_1^0$ to be winning for White, $\psi(p)=0$. Also,

$$
T_1^1=\begin{aligned}&\{(q,1)\mid\exists p\in T_0,A((q,1),p)\wedge\psi(p)=1\}\cup\\
&\{(q,0)\mid\forall p\in T_0(A((q,0),p)\to\psi(p)=1)\}\end{aligned}
$$

These positions are all winning for Black, $\psi(p)=1$. Repeating this definition yields all winning positions. For positions won't be reached, we define $\psi(p)=2$, meaning drawn/undefined.<br><br>
We do not need to handle limit cases separately, because any position that is winning must terminate in finitely many moves. One can reach each limit stage in finitely many steps. This conclusion is a consequence of the well-ordering of the ordinals.<br><br>
Needless to say, the $\psi$ defined in this way is $\Sigma_1$-definable.
> Note that recursion itself contributes to complexity. Here, however, our recursion is only over $\omega$, so it is at most $\Sigma_1$ complexity and does not cause much influence. Later, when defining the game value, the complexity of the recursion itself will have a significant effect.

#### Winning-play tree $T_p$
For a White-winning position $\psi(p)=0$, define the tree of winning play starting from $p$. First recursively define sequences: $T_p^0=\{(p)\}$. Assume $T_p^i$ has been defined, we define $T_p^{i+1}$ by:

$$
T_p^{i+1}=\{(\textbf i,q)\mid\textbf i=(p,p_1,\cdots,p_i)\in T_p^i\wedge q\in T\wedge A(p_i,q)\wedge\psi(q)=1\wedge\forall s\leq i(p_i\neq q)\}.
$$

Finally take

$$
T_p'=\{s\mid\exists i\in\omega(s\in T_p^i)\wedge s_i\in T_0\}.
$$

Clearly, $T_p'$ is in fact the set of all winning lines from $p$. Since every concrete line must end in finitely many steps, the collection is complete. At the same time, it excludes “back-and-forth shuffling.” Next we reorganize it into a tree.<br><br>
Now define the partial order $<$, and define the set of elements of the tree $T_p^*$:

$$
T_p^*=\{(q,s\upharpoonright_i)\mid s\in T_p'\wedge\exists i\in\operatorname{dom}(s)\wedge s(i)=q\}.
$$

Then define $<$ as follows:

$$
(q,s)<(m,r)\leftrightarrow s\subset r\wedge q\in\operatorname{rng}(r).
$$

Clearly $(p,\varnothing)$ is the minimum element, and the set of all smaller elements is well-ordered. In this way it can be organized into a tree.<br><br>
Naturally, this is $\Sigma_1$-definable.
### Ordinal game value $\operatorname{cf}$
For each White-winning position $p\in T$ with $\psi(p)=0$, the ordinal game value $\operatorname{cf}(p)$ is the minimum number of moves required to win under optimal play. It can be defined easily using $T_p$. Starting from the top nodes can be found,these nodes are all in $T_0$, and their game value is $0$. Recursively define upstream nodes $(q,s)$: if $\operatorname{cf}$ has been defined for all successor nodes of $q$, then $q$ can also be defined.
- If $q$ is a position with White to move, define $\operatorname{cf}(q)=\bigcap\limits_{\forall r\in\operatorname{dom}T_p(A(q,r))}\operatorname{cf}(r)+1$
- If $q$ is a position with Black to move, define $\operatorname{cf}(q)=\bigcup\limits_{\forall r\in\operatorname{dom}T_p(A(q,r))}\operatorname{cf}(r)$

By the definition of $T_p$, if there is another node $(q,r)$, its game value will agree with that of $(q,s)$, so we may write $\operatorname{cf}(q)$, ignoring $s$.<br><br>
Clearly, $\operatorname{cf}$ is $\Sigma_1$-definable.

---

With the preparation complete, we now come to the main body of the proof.
### Proof
First consider a model $V$ of $ZF$ and its inner model $L$. Since $L$ is a model of $ZFC+GCH$, and since infinite chess is an open game, the usual $ZFC$ analysis of game values applies inside $L$.<br><br>
Fix a position $p\in T$ that has a game value, obviously a White winning situation. Consider the statement

$$
\varphi: \forall p\in T,\|\operatorname{cf}(p)\|=\omega
$$

It's easy to see that $\varphi$ is a $\Sigma_1$ statement. The only stuff need to point out is that the definition of cardinality. Cardinality says there exists a 1-1 map function from $\|\operatorname{cf}(p)\|$ to $\omega$, also $\Sigma_1$-definable<br><br>
In $L$, by $ZFC+GCH$ and the standard theory of ordinal game values for open games, $\varphi$ holds: every winning position has a countable ordinal game value. Therefore, by the upward absoluteness of $\Sigma_1$ statements from the transitive inner model $L$ to $V$, the statement $\varphi$ also holds in $V$.<br><br>
Since $p$ was an arbitrary winning position, it follows that in any model of $ZF$, every game value that exists is a countable ordinal. Hence no game value can reach $\omega_1$. This disproves the conjecture that removing the Axiom of Choice allows game values to attain $\omega_1$.

---

By this proof, we can find some intersting result. In previous, we researched the game value with regularity of $\omega_1$ and CH, but by this proof, we can find out acctually we can abort it and study it in just $ZF$ and all statements will be held as usual.
